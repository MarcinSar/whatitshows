What each one shows

parser: the shared parser reads the month first

The report shows a day and a month swapped: “Our 7 April reminder went out on 4 July.” The tenant writes the day first, and the report came in by app, by email, or through a booking-v2 form, whose hint asks for DD/MM/YYYY. People typed the date their way. The shared parser read it month first. This is the bug #4471 names. Fixed on 5 October 2026: fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales.

hint: booking-v3’s form asks for the month first

The report shows the same swap, so it sounds the same. It only comes through the booking form, at day-first tenants that run booking-v3. That form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field, so people type the month first, and the form then reads the date the tenant’s way, day first. A parser fix does not reach it. Not planned: the booking-v3 form was retired, so no new reports can come this way.

one-day: the date moves by one day

The report shows no swap. “Invite says Tuesday 12 May. Calendar says Monday 11 May.” “Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday. Same meeting.” “The invite arrived before I made the booking.” The day moves by one, or differs between time zones: a time-zone conversion of the date, not its order. Every one comes from an EU tenant that writes the day first, by app or email. The desk already noted these reports as a one-day move (desk-notes.json), and still counts them under parser by tenant. Open: what they show is a different symptom, and the dd/mm fix does not explain it. It stays open until it is confirmed: one-day reports that arrive after 5 October would confirm it.

How the reports were split

  • One report counts once. The export comes in pages that overlap, so the same report can appear twice; it is counted by id.
  • The cause comes from the tenant (its locale and its booking form) and the channel, as causes.ts decides it. Not from the intake tag, which is a guess from the reporter’s browser, and not from the subject line, which is how everything ended up in #4471.
  • Reports the desk noted as a one-day move are their own problem, whichever tenant sent them.
  • The counts follow the desk’s own view, the cause table on the #4471 page: every report merged into #4471 is filed under one cause, by tenant, and counted there. The one-day reports keep their place under parser and are counted again in their own row. The table checks that the causes add up to the ticket.

The counts come from the data at render time. When the desk merges more reports into #4471, or notes another one-day move, render the page again; the counts follow.
